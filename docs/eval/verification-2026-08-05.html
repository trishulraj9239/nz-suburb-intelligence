NZ Suburb Intelligence · verification report · 5 Aug 2026

What the eval actually proves — and what it doesn’t

Four full runs of the 22-question suite against the live /api/ask pipeline, plus a hand audit of the results. The deterministic gate holds. One structural gap is real. And one alarm I raised earlier turns out not to be supported — the blind judge was wrong, not the app.

How a question is scored

Two independent layers, and only one of them is deterministic

Every question is put to both models through the real server — same planner, same execution, same citation contract as production. Nothing is mocked.

Layer 1 — deterministic checks, in run.mjs

  • plan_valid — the planner returned a structured plan rather than failing.
  • citations_ok — every {{cN}} marker resolves to a row the server actually returned.
  • refusal_ok — an unanswerable question declines instead of inventing.
  • source_match — where a question names a source, a cited row comes from it.

Layer 2 — a blind judge

Claude Opus 4.8 scores each answer 1–5 on grounded · cited · hedged · concise · overall, without knowing which model produced it. It is a different model from the one under test — but it is still a language model, and this report shows it making confident false accusations.

Evidence — one question, audited by hand

A trap question, working correctly

verified live

q14-dwelling-trap asks for something the data cannot supply. The right answer gives the figure that does exist and names the limitation.

Question

“What is the median rent for a two-bedroom apartment in Takapuna?”

Rows the server handed the model

[1] Takapuna Central — Median rent (new tenancies): 675 $/week
[2] Takapuna Central — Upper-quartile rent: 1214 $/week
[3] Takapuna Central — Lower-quartile rent: 581 $/week

Answer

The MBIE tenancy bond data shows an all-dwellings median rent of approximately $675/week c1, covering new tenancies across all dwelling types and bedroom counts — it cannot be broken down to two-bedroom apartments specifically. The lower quartile is approximately $581/week c3 and the upper quartile approximately $1,214/week c2, giving you a sense of the spread, though confidence on these figures is low.

Evidence — a disputed score

The judge accused the model of fabricating. It hadn’t.

my earlier alarm: not supported

q22-buyer-open scored 1/5. On that basis I told you answers were inventing figures and slipping past the gate. I re-ran the question and checked it against the rows myself.

What the judge wrote

“Fabricates intensification-capacity percentages and suburb names (New Lynn, Henderson, Westgate) that appear nowhere in the rows, with citations pointing to entirely different data.”

Rows actually returned

[1] Māngere Mascot — Intensification capacity: 100%
[2] Tamaki West — Intensification capacity: 100%
[3] Ferguson — Intensification capacity: 100%
[4] Wesley West — Intensification capacity: 100%
[5] New Lynn Central South — Intensification capacity: 100%
[6] Henderson Lincoln South — Intensification capacity: 100%
[7] Wesley South — Intensification capacity: 100%
[8] Takanini McLennan — Intensification capacity: 100%
[9] Hobsonville Point Catalina Bay — Intensification capacity: 100%
[10] Ellerslie West — Intensification capacity: 100%

The answer it produced

All ten suburbs shown here share approximately 100% intensification capacity under the Unitary Plan c1…c10, meaning their entire zoned land base is flagged as having redevelopment potential… To differentiate further… it would be worth comparing consents activity, flood hazard exposure, and median household income — but that data isn’t available in the rows provided here.

New Lynn and Henderson are rows 5 and 6. The percentages are the rows’ own values. The answer even declines to go further because the other data isn’t present — the opposite of fabricating. The judge invented the fabrication.

Caveat, stated precisely: the scored run and my re-run are different invocations, and the harness does not persist the rows it judged — so I cannot inspect the exact rows behind the 1/5. That inability is itself a finding (issue 3 below). What is certain is that two of the three “fabricated” names are real rows in a live run of the same question, and that a 1/5 here is not reproducible.

The real gap

“Citations 22/22” does not mean the numbers are right

proven by construction

This one holds regardless of what the judge says, because it’s a property of the code. The check in scripts/eval/run.mjs is:

markers.every(n => sourceNs.has(n))

It asks whether the marker resolves to a row. It never compares the number written beside the marker to that row’s value. I fed the real Takapuna rows to the same logic with deliberately corrupted answers:

Answer fed to the scorerWhat’s wrong with itcitations_ok
Median rent is $675/wk c1 nothing — correct true
Median rent is $9,999/wk c1 figure invented; row 1 is 675 true
The lower quartile is $1,214/wk c3 cites the lower-quartile row for the upper-quartile figure true

So the headline metric means “every marker pointed at a real row”, not “every figure was grounded”. No evidence yet that the model exploits this — both answers I audited were correct — but the gate cannot currently tell the difference, and it’s the project’s flagship honesty claim.

Issues

Four problems, ranked by how solid the evidence is

1 · The citation check can’t detect a wrong number

proven

Demonstrated above by construction. Fix: compare the numeric token preceding each marker against the cited row’s value, normalising for $ / % / min / commas and allowing the rounding the prompt permits. Report it as a separate values_grounded metric. Expect it to lower historical scores — that’s the point.

2 · The judge is noisy, and sometimes simply wrong

verified twice

Identical code scored one question 1 and then 5. A 1/5 “fabrication” verdict was contradicted by direct inspection. At one sample per question, quality deltas between runs are not measurable — which is why I am not claiming the recent NL fix improved quality, only that it fixed two specific dead ends. Fix: score each question a few times and take a median, or treat the judge as triage that a deterministic check adjudicates.

3 · Results don’t persist the rows that were judged

proven

latest.json stores the answer and the score but not the source rows, so a disputed judgement cannot be re-examined after the fact — exactly the situation above. Fix: persist the rows alongside the answer. Cheap, and it makes every future score auditable.

4 · Two judge calls fail with an API 400

reproducible

q16-safety-trap fails consistently; the question then carries no score and silently drops out of the average, which quietly shifts the headline number. Fix alongside issue 2.

Recommendation

Fix the scorer before trusting the next gate

The M17 checkpoint is specified as “full suite ≥ baseline”. Passing that gate today would demonstrate less than it appears to: the deterministic half can’t catch a wrong figure, and the judge half can’t be relied on question by question. Issues 1 and 3 are small, deterministic pieces of work that make every subsequent milestone’s verification mean something.
